What is Regenerative Agraculture?

Not a single Method

Regenerative agriculture is not a fixed recipe — practices are tailored to local climate, soil, and farm goals. Its a framework for making the land, community, and business healthier year after year

Regenerative agriculture is a conservation and rehabilitation approach to food and farming systems. It focuses on topsoil regeneration, increasing biodiversity, enhancing ecosystem services, supporting biosequestration, increasing resilience to climate change, and strengthening the health and vitality of farm soil.

Regenerative agriculture is not a specific practice. It combines a variety of sustainable agriculture techniques. Practices include maximal recycling of farm waste and adding composted material from non-farm sources.

Regenerative agriculture claims to mitigate climate change through carbon dioxide removal from the atmosphere and sequestration. Carbon sequestration is gaining popularity in agriculture from individuals as well as groups. However, such claims have also been subject to criticism by scientists.

Core Principles

  • Improves soil health through minimal disturbance, organic matter addition, and living roots in the ground
  • Boosts biodiversity by integrating diverse crops, trees, and animals
  • Enhances resilience to climate change and farm stressors
  • Supports carbon sequestration by storing CO₂ in soil and vegetation
  • Reduces reliance on synthetic inputs while still using them where necessary

Key Practices

  • Minimizing soil disturbance (no-till, direct drilling, no-dig)
  • Cover cropping and diverse rotations to protect soil and add nutrients
  • Integrating livestock through adaptive grazing or pasture diversity
  • Agroforestry and tree planting for habitat and carbon storage
  • Composting and organic amendments to enrich soil
  • Mulching and sheet mulching to suppress weeds and retain moisture

Why It Matters

  • Addresses soil degradation affecting over half of the world’s farmland
  • Can reduce greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture if scaled globally
  • Supports long-term farm profitability and ecosystem health
    • Combines indigenous knowledge with modern science for sustainable outcomes
